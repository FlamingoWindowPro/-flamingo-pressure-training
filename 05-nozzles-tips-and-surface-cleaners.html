Module 5 — Nozzles, Tips & Surface Cleaner Attachments

Match the right tip and attachment to the surface and task in front of you.

5.15.2

5.1 — Standard Color-Coded Tips

  • Red (0°) — pinpoint jet; concrete spot-stains only, never siding or wood
  • Yellow (15°) — narrow fan, used sparingly on tough concrete stains
  • Green (25°) — general-purpose fan for concrete and hard surfaces
  • White (40°) — wider, gentler fan for siding and general rinsing
  • Black (65°) — very wide, low-impact, used with detergent at low pressure
  • Soap tip — always low pressure to apply detergent, never to rinse

5.2 — Surface Cleaner Attachments

A surface cleaner is a rotating dual- or tri-nozzle head used for flat hard surfaces like driveways and patios. It delivers even, streak-free coverage far faster than a hand wand.

  • Keep the surface cleaner flat against the surface at all times
  • Overlap each pass by a few inches to avoid faint stripe lines
  • Move at a steady, consistent walking pace
  • Never use on wood decking unless specifically rated for it

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using a red 0° tip on siding or wood
  • Tilting the surface cleaner instead of keeping it flat
  • Stopping in place with the surface cleaner running, causing etching
  • Guessing at a tip instead of checking the color chart

💬 Real-World Scenario

A technician can't remember which tip to use on an unfamiliar surface. What's the rule of thumb?

Choose the widest angle and lowest pressure that could reasonably work, then adjust up only after testing in an inconspicuous spot.
