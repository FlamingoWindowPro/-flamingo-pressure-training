Module 8 — House & Siding Washing Techniques

Apply the correct prep and technique across the different siding materials technicians will encounter.

8.18.28.3

8.1 — Property Prep Walkthrough

  • Walk the full perimeter with the customer or photograph it first, noting existing damage
  • Close all windows and doors; confirm outlets are covered or avoided
  • Move or cover furniture, grills, and decorations near the wash area
  • Pre-wet landscaping along the entire wash path

8.2 — By Siding Type

  • Vinyl: soft wash preferred; if using pressure, never spray upward into panel seams
  • Brick: moderate pressure tolerated, avoid direct contact with mortar joints
  • Stucco: low pressure only — porous and easily gouged or cracked
  • Wood siding/shingles: soft wash strongly preferred

8.3 — Common Problem Areas

  • Corners and butt joints — algae builds up faster in shaded, low-airflow areas
  • Areas beneath gutters and downspouts
  • Window and door trim — easy to over-pressure and damage caulking
  • Areas near dryer vents — need extra dwell time, not extra pressure

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Spraying upward into vinyl siding seams
  • Applying full pressure directly to stucco
  • Rinsing bottom to top instead of top to bottom
  • Skipping the pre-job perimeter walkthrough

💬 Real-World Scenario

A technician notices mortar starting to look worn on an older brick home mid-job. What should they do?

Back off pressure immediately and avoid direct contact with the mortar joints going forward — flag the existing wear to the customer before continuing.
