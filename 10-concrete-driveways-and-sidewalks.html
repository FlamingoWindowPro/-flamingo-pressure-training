Module 10 — Concrete, Driveways & Sidewalks

Produce even, streak-free hard-surface results and know when a stain needs to be escalated.

10.110.210.3

10.1 — Pre-Treatment

  • Sweep or blow off loose debris before starting
  • Pre-treat heavy oil or rust stains with degreaser or rust remover and allow dwell time
  • Note existing cracks, spalling, or discoloration and point them out to the customer first

10.2 — Washing Technique

  • Use a surface cleaner for open, flat areas; hand-wand only tight corners and edges
  • Work in overlapping passes at a steady pace to prevent striping
  • Rinse from the highest point toward drainage, keeping chemical out of storm drains where prohibited
  • For heavy stains, a second detergent pass beats holding the wand in place longer

10.3 — When to Escalate

  • Stains remaining after two properly dwelled passes may need a specialty product — check with your lead
  • Efflorescence (white mineral staining) needs an acid-based treatment outside standard scope
  • Large cracks, heaving, or unstable surfaces should be flagged, not walked with heavy equipment

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Holding the wand in one spot to fight a stubborn stain instead of a second detergent pass
  • Moving the surface cleaner unevenly, causing stripes
  • Not sweeping debris before starting
  • Missing existing cracks during the pre-job walkthrough

💬 Real-World Scenario

A driveway has visible stripes after a technician finishes. What likely caused it?

Uneven surface cleaner speed or overlap. The fix is a slower, more consistent pace with proper pass overlap — not more pressure.
