Module 3 — Pressure Washing Equipment Overview

Build fluency with the equipment on the truck before it's used on a real property.

3.1 — Core Components

  • Pump — generates pressure (PSI) and flow (GPM)
  • Engine or motor — gas for high-output rigs, electric for smaller jobs
  • Hose and reel — high-pressure rated, typically 100–200 ft
  • Wand and lance — trigger, tip, and often a chemical injector
  • Nozzles/tips — interchangeable spray patterns (Module 5)
  • Downstream and X-jet injectors — draw and apply detergent at low pressure

3.2 — Cold Water vs. Hot Water Units

Flamingo's standard trucks run cold-water systems, sufficient for most residential work. Hot water units cut through grease faster and are reserved for commercial pads — technicians on hot-water rigs receive separate training.

3.3 — Pre-Trip Inspection

This should take less than five minutes and happen before every job, not just the first job of the week.

  • Check engine oil and fuel, or confirm GFCI-protected power
  • Inspect the high-pressure hose for cracks, bulges, or leaks
  • Confirm the wand's trigger lock functions and the tip isn't worn
  • Check the chemical injector and tank for cracks or residue
  • Test water supply flow before starting the pump
  • Confirm the surface cleaner attachment spins freely

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Skipping the pre-trip inspection because the rig 'was fine yesterday'
  • Running the pump dry while chasing a hose kink
  • Not knowing which injector applies chemical vs. rinses
  • Ignoring a hose that's starting to bulge at a fitting

💬 Real-World Scenario

A technician notices the high-pressure hose has a small bulge near a fitting. What should they do?

Stop and flag it before the job starts — a bulging hose can fail suddenly under pressure. Swap it out; don't run it 'just this once.'
