Module 4 — Understanding PSI, GPM & Water Pressure

Give technicians the judgment to choose the right pressure for the surface in front of them, not just a memorized number.

4.14.24.3

4.1 — PSI vs. GPM

PSI measures the force of the water stream. GPM measures the volume of water moving through the system. Cleaning power is a product of both — high PSI with low GPM cuts a narrow, intense line, while higher GPM helps rinse and cover ground faster.

4.2 — Pressure Ranges by Surface

  • Vinyl siding: 1,200–1,900 PSI, wide fan tip, soft-wash preferred
  • Brick and stucco: 1,500–2,500 PSI depending on condition
  • Concrete/driveways: 3,000–4,000 PSI, often with a surface cleaner
  • Wood decking and fencing: 500–1,200 PSI — easily gouged at higher settings
  • Roof shingles: soft wash only — never direct high pressure

4.3 — Signs of Too Much Pressure

  • Wood fibers lifting or 'fuzzing' on decking
  • Paint or caulking peeling from siding or trim
  • Mortar lines eroding on brick
  • Etching or 'zebra stripes' on concrete

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using one pressure setting for every surface on a job
  • Skipping the test-spot check on an unfamiliar surface
  • Assuming higher pressure always means a faster, better result
  • Not recognizing early signs of pressure damage until it's severe

💬 Real-World Scenario

A technician isn't sure what pressure a weathered wood fence can handle. What's the safest approach?

Start at the lowest pressure and widest tip that could plausibly work, test in an inconspicuous spot, and only step up if needed. It's always easier to add pressure than undo damage.
