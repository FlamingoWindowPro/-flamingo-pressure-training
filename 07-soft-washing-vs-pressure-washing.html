Module 7 — Soft Washing vs. Pressure Washing

Teach the single distinction that prevents the most expensive technician mistakes.

7.17.27.3

7.1 — What Is Soft Washing?

Soft washing uses a low-pressure application (often under 100 PSI at the surface) of a chemical solution to kill algae, mold, mildew, and organic staining, followed by a low-to-moderate pressure rinse. The chemical does the work — the water applies and removes it.

7.2 — When Soft Washing Is Required

  • Roof shingles — always
  • Delicate or aged siding, stucco, and painted surfaces
  • Any surface where the manufacturer specifies low-pressure only

7.3 — Basic Soft-Wash Process

  • Pre-wet all landscaping and sensitive surfaces
  • Apply diluted solution bottom to top, low pressure
  • Allow proper dwell time — typically 5–15 minutes
  • Do not let the solution fully dry before rinsing
  • Rinse top to bottom with clean water
  • Re-rinse all landscaping thoroughly

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Pressure washing a roof instead of soft washing it
  • Letting chemical solution dry on the surface before rinsing
  • Applying solution top to bottom instead of bottom to top
  • Skipping the landscaping re-rinse

💬 Real-World Scenario

A customer asks why their roof streaking isn't being 'blasted off' with high pressure like their driveway. How should the technician explain it?

Explain that roofs are soft-washed, not pressure washed — high pressure strips shingle granules and can void the manufacturer's warranty, while the soft-wash chemical kills the algae causing the streaking without any mechanical force.
