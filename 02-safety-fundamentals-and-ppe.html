Module 2 — Safety Fundamentals & Personal Protective Equipment

Build the hazard awareness and PPE habits every later module depends on.

2.12.22.32.4

2.1 — Core Hazards

Walk through each hazard type and, where possible, share a real near-miss story.

  • High-pressure water injection injuries — the stream can break skin at close range
  • Chemical exposure — sodium hypochlorite (SH) and surfactants can burn skin/eyes and harm plants
  • Slip-and-fall — wet surfaces, hoses, and soap create serious footing hazards
  • Electrical hazards — water near outdoor outlets and light fixtures
  • Falls from height — roof and second-story siding work
  • Kickback and hose whip — sudden loss of nozzle control under pressure

2.2 — Required PPE

Every technician is issued and fitted with their own set before their first supervised job.

  • Chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile, not latex, for SH-based mixes)
  • Safety glasses or goggles whenever chemicals or a wand are in use
  • Non-slip, closed-toe footwear — no sandals, ever
  • Long pants and a chemical-resistant apron/jacket for concentrated detergent
  • Hearing protection for extended gas-engine use
  • Fall protection harness and anchor points above 6 feet

2.3 — Never Do These Things

  • Never point a wand at yourself, a coworker, or a bystander
  • Never mix bleach-based detergents with anything containing ammonia
  • Never bypass a safety lock or trigger lock on a wand
  • Never work from a ladder holding a live pressure hose without a second technician managing it

2.4 — Emergency Response

Every truck carries an eyewash bottle and the nearest urgent care address. If chemical gets in someone's eyes, flush immediately with the eyewash bottle for at least 15 minutes and call for medical guidance — do not wait to see if it clears on its own.

MODULE 2 CERTIFICATION: No technician handles a pressure washer, mixes a chemical, or works from a ladder until this module is signed off.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Skipping PPE because 'it's just a quick job'
  • Assuming safety glasses are optional when only rinsing with plain water
  • Not knowing where the truck's eyewash bottle is before starting work
  • Treating a trigger lock as a nuisance instead of a safety feature

💬 Real-World Scenario

A technician splashes a small amount of mixed SH solution near their eyes. What's the very first thing they should do?

Flush the eyes immediately with the truck's eyewash bottle for at least 15 minutes and seek medical guidance — don't wait to see if irritation passes on its own.
