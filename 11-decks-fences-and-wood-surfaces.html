Module 11 — Decks, Fences & Wood Surfaces

Clean and brighten wood without damaging the grain or missing wood that needs repair, not washing.

11.111.211.3

11.1 — Assessing the Wood First

  • Softwoods (cedar, pine) are more easily gouged than hardwoods
  • Weathered, graying wood is more fragile than recently sealed wood
  • Loose boards, popped nails, or soft/rotted sections should be flagged, not washed over

11.2 — Technique

  • Keep pressure in the 500–1,200 PSI range with a wide fan tip
  • Always wash with the grain, never across it
  • Keep the tip at a consistent distance — moving closer to 'dig out' dirt causes gouging
  • A wood brightener restores tone on grey wood without extra pressure

11.3 — Fences

Fence washing follows the same principles as decking. Pay attention to areas near sprinklers and shaded fence lines, which build up algae faster, and the base of posts where soil splash-back accumulates.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Washing across the grain instead of with it
  • Moving the tip too close to 'dig out' embedded dirt
  • Continuing after wood fibers start to fuzz or lift
  • Washing over a board that should have been flagged for repair

💬 Real-World Scenario

A technician notices wood fibers starting to fuzz while washing a deck. What should they do?

Back off immediately — this means the pressure is too high or the tip is too close. Widen the tip angle, increase distance, or lower pressure before continuing.
